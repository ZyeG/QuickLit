The Social Impact of Generative AI: An Analysis on ChatGPT
ABSTRACT: The paper conducts a systematic literature review on the societal implications of generative AI—focusing on ChatGPT—synthesizing reported benefits, risks, emerging trends, and areas for improvement. It aims to inform policy, regulation, and responsible, human-centered AI development.

1 Introduction: The authors frame the rapid rise of ChatGPT within broader debates about AI’s benefits and harms, noting optimism, skepticism, and critical concerns around democracy, inequality, and security. They motivate a comprehensive, structured analysis and outline the paper’s organization.

2 BACKGROUND: The section situates generative AI within historical patterns of technological disruption and competing societal narratives (optimistic, skeptical, and critical). It underscores why ChatGPT warrants study given its potential to alter social dynamics and public interest surges following its late‑2022 release.

3 STATE OF THE ART: The authors review the evolution from transformers to large language models and generative pre‑training, then overview prominent foundational models (e.g., GPT‑4, PaLM, LLaMA). They describe task‑specific adaptation (e.g., instruction‑tuning, chatbots) and multimodal capabilities, highlighting the ecosystem of domain‑focused tools.

4 STUDY DESIGN: Using established guidelines for systematic reviews, the study defines three research questions on perceived impacts, emerging trends, and improvement areas. The search spans grey literature and academic sources with explicit strings, applies eligibility criteria, and details a multi‑stage screening and data‑extraction process.

5 DATA ANALYSIS AND SYNTHESIS: Seventeen hundred-plus initial hits narrowed to 1,316 items (1,230 grey; 86 academic), from which 71 publications were ultimately selected. Using Atlas.ti, texts were coded into four groups—positive impacts, negative impacts, emerging trends, and areas for improvement—then visualized (e.g., treemaps, Sankey) to reveal frequency patterns and distribution across document types. Recurrent codes included bias and disinformation risks on the negative side, and benefits for education and customer service on the positive side.

6 Discussion: Positive perceptions emphasize 24/7 assistance, automation of routine tasks, and value in education, customer service, healthcare, journalism, and cross‑cultural communication. Salient concerns include bias and discrimination, misinformation/disinformation, privacy and data‑protection issues (including minors and surveillance), and potential job displacement, with implications for democratic processes. The section also notes worries about over‑ or under‑regulation and highlights emerging issues such as liability, copyright uncertainty, and limits from out‑of‑date knowledge. Overall, it portrays a mixed but cautionary landscape that varies by sector and document type.

7 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK: The review identifies privacy, bias, and inequality as urgent challenges while acknowledging substantial benefits across sectors. It calls for robust governance, ethics, and context‑aware regulation that prevent harm without stifling innovation, and proposes comparative analyses of ChatGPT regulation across the U.S., Europe, and Latin America as future work.
