QONNECT: A QoS-Aware Orchestration System for Distributed Kubernetes Clusters
1 Introduction: Motivates the need for QoS-aware orchestration across the cloud–fog–edge continuum, noting that vanilla Kubernetes lacks native support for cross-cluster, objective-driven placement. Introduces QONNECT and outlines contributions: a vendor-agnostic architecture, a weighted Borda-count scheduler for energy/cost/performance goals, and a Raft-based fault-tolerant control plane.

2 Background: Reviews Kubernetes and lightweight distributions (k3s), consensus via Raft for consistent multicluster control, and preference-based scheduling with Borda count to encode QoS objectives. Sets the technical context for building a distributed, QoS-aware orchestrator.

3 Architecture: Defines requirements for user-guided domain placement, QoS-driven deployment, disruption recovery, and fault tolerance. Presents a Clean Architecture–inspired design with a global Knowledge Base, Raft-replicated Resource Lead Agents (RLAs) that take scheduling decisions, and per-cluster Resource Agents (RAs) that enact deployments and stream telemetry. Details an implementation where the leader RLA periodically schedules using a multi-step Borda pipeline (eligibility filtering, per-attribute ranking, capacity aggregation, QoS weighting, and cluster aggregation) and exposes a REST API with manifest portability conventions. Describes RA workflows (registration, node snapshots, config polling, workload application, health heartbeats, and cleanup) enabling automated placement, migration, and failover across domains.

4 Evaluation: Uses a nine-cluster kind-based testbed (cloud/fog/edge × energy-, cost-, performance-efficient profiles) with representative energy, cost, and bandwidth parameters and the Istio Bookinfo app mapped across domains. Experiments show QONNECT initially places services per a chosen QoS priority (e.g., performance), then automatically re-deploys them when QoS goals change (e.g., to energy), and handles failures by migrating workloads and re-electing leaders without manual intervention. A comparative analysis indicates QONNECT combines continuum support, QoS-aware scheduling, distributed control, and self-healing beyond what prior systems typically offer.

5 Related Work: Surveys orchestration across the continuum (e.g., MiCADO-Edge, ACOA, Nautilus, KubeEdge-based approaches) and highlights their focuses and limitations regarding QoS policies, distribution, and failover. Positions QONNECT as integrating QoS-driven, distributed scheduling with self-healing across heterogeneous clusters.

6 Conclusion and Future Work: Concludes that QONNECT operationalizes intent-based QoS vectors into concrete multicluster placements and migrations with a resilient Raft control plane, demonstrating automatic recovery and policy-driven behavior on a federated testbed. Notes limitations of synthetic conditions and outlines future directions including real-world deployments, richer QoS dimensions (especially latency), and telemetry-driven energy models with predictive migration.
