Towards a Decentralised Application-Centric Orchestration Framework in the Cloud-Edge Continuum

Introduction: Presents the challenges of orchestrating distributed applications across the Cloud-Edge continuum and limitations of centralized solutions. Introduces Swarmchestrate and outlines contributions: a decentralized architecture, a simulation-based proof of concept, a resource-offer collection strategy, several selection algorithms, and an evaluation focused on deployment.

Related Work: Reviews centralized, hierarchical, slice-based, and peer-to-peer orchestration approaches and their trade-offs. Positions Swarmchestrate as a hybrid, highly decentralized, application-centric model with a two-layer structure and P2P mechanisms, where resources are passively discovered based on application needs.

Swarmchestrate: Proposed Approach: Describes an architecture with an Application view (TOSCA descriptions covering components, resource needs, QoS goals, monitoring), a Resource view (provider resources grouped into “Capacities”), a distributed Knowledge Management layer, and a decentralized Orchestration Space. Resource Agents form a P2P interface that represents capacities and collaborates to discover resources. Deployment proceeds by collecting resource offers, ranking them using QoS priorities and reliability to form a Swarm, instantiating a lead resource with a Kubernetes control plane and lead Swarm Agent, and then launching application components and additional SAs with high-availability configurations. Intelligence is embedded via optimization and learning to drive selection and future reconfiguration.

Evaluation: Implements a first study in the DISSECT-CF-Fog simulator (extended with RAs and capacities), submitting six heterogeneous applications onto eight capacities represented by a network of eight RAs. Offers are generated by combining RA–component mappings and ranked using two methods (a weighted cost function and Borda voting), each with additive or multiplicative reliability. Metrics include simulation time, total price, average deployment time, and total energy; priority-aware strategies consistently improve their targeted objectives, with the cost-based method generally achieving the best priority-specific values and bandwidth priority minimizing deployment time. The results indicate feasibility and highlight trade-offs among latency, bandwidth, cost, and energy under different ranking strategies.

Conclusion: Summarizes Swarmchestrate as a decentralized, application-centric orchestration framework that enables seamless deployment across heterogeneous providers, showing promise in simulation. Ongoing work targets full implementation, runtime self-organization for reconfiguration, and prototyping on four real-world use cases.
